Professional Standards Checklist

For digital evidence to be admissible in a court of law, investigators must strictly adhere to the guidelines set by ISO/IEC 27037 (Guidelines for identification, collection, acquisition, and preservation of digital evidence) and NIST SP 800-86 (Guide to Integrating Forensic Techniques into Incident Response).

Phase 1: Identification & Protection of Scene Standards Compliant

Pinpoint the potential source devices and secure the physical scene. Avoid changing the state of any device (do not turn running computers off or non-running computers on without justification).

  • Establish physical perimeter and secure target digital devices.
  • Photograph physical interfaces, cable hookups, and screen contents before touching.
  • Block signal connectivity (activate airplane mode, insert in Faraday bags) to prevent remote wipes.
Phase 2: Acquisition & Cryptographic Hashing In Progress

Duplicate the storage medium bit-for-bit to create a forensic image (E01 or RAW). A write-blocker must be used to prevent write cycles on target media. Calculate SHA-256 hash immediately.

  • Connect target hard drive/USB to forensic workstation using a hardware write-blocker (e.g. Tableau T8u).
  • Create bit-stream clone using FTK Imager, dd, or EnCase, storing it in E01 / RAW format.
  • Generate original SHA-256 acquisition hash and record it in the case register.
Phase 3: Custody & Secure Storage Pending

Establish the Chain of Custody ledger. Store evidence in secure, anti-static storage lockers with key-card logs. Document every transfer in real-time.

  • Label device with case number, investigator initials, and unique item number.
  • Seal item in anti-static forensic bag with tamper-evident tape.
  • Log initial acquisition transfer details in the Chain of Custody tracking system.
Phase 4: Analysis & Final Court Admissibility Report Pending

Analyze the forensic clones (never analyze original media!). Before beginning research, re-verify baseline hashes. Create a final report summarizing timeline of events.

  • Re-hash evidence files and compare against original baseline to prove no data modifications.
  • Search forensic images using Autopsy, Axiom, or Volatility for artifacts (deleted files, registry keys).
  • Compile findings, case notes, and Chain of Custody audit logs into the final legal report.
